Fritti

Home: Atlanta, Georgia
Style: Neapolitan
The meatball: crumbled, Inman Park
Signature pie: Meatball pie

The directory files Fritti of Atlanta, GA under Neapolitan — and files it there because of one pie: Meatball pie.

It is the standard Fritti was built on in Atlanta, and the one its regulars enforce. The Neapolitan school is a discipline: a soft, leopard-spotted crust from a fierce oven, San Marzano-style tomato, and restraint everywhere. Meatballs here are used sparingly, the way Naples would insist. That is how Fritti keeps it in Atlanta.

Here the meatball is crumbled, Inman Park. Crumbling is the generous method: meat in every bite, edges that crisp against the cheese, and a pie that reads as a meatball pie from edge to tip rather than a cheese pie with guests. In Atlanta, Fritti would not serve it any other way.

The pie to know is Meatball pie. Everything else on the menu orbits it here; this is the order that fills the room's recommendation when a stranger asks what to get. It is the Fritti way in Atlanta, and the regulars hold them to it.

Shops like this one hold a neighborhood together: the birthday pie, the Friday pie, the pie after the game. The meatball version is the connoisseur's standing order among them. In Atlanta, Fritti would not serve it any other way.

At Fritti in Atlanta, the point deserves underlining. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.
