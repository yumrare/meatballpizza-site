Burt's Place

Home: Morton Grove, Illinois
Style: Chicago deep dish
The meatball: crumbled, Burt Katz's pan pies
Signature pie: Meatball pan pie
On the record since: 1989

Burt's Place is Morton Grove's entry in the meatball pizza record — a Chicago deep dish kitchen in IL where the test pie is the one topped with meatballs.

At Burt's Place in Morton Grove, that standard is not theory — it is the nightly measure. Deep dish is a construction, not a crust with ambitions: a buttery pan walls in layers of cheese and filling under chunky tomato, and it is eaten with a knife and fork without apology. Burt's Place keeps that standard in Morton Grove, night after night.

The preparation is crumbled, Burt Katz's pan pies — the kitchen treats its meatball mix as a topping in its own right, crumbling it over the sauce so the pie eats like a baked meatball parmigiana in slice form. Burt's Place keeps that standard in Morton Grove, night after night.

Meatball pan pie is the signature — the pie that carries the house meatball at full strength, built on the crust the shop is known for and finished the way the regulars expect. Burt's Place keeps that standard in Morton Grove, night after night.

On the record since 1989. Burt Katz's Morton Grove room became a pilgrimage under Anthony Bourdain's spotlight — pan pies ordered ahead, caramelized crust, a lifetime's method.

The Burt's Place kitchen in Morton Grove treats this as doctrine. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.

On IL's pizza map, Morton Grove is not a pass-through — it is a pie town, and rooms like Burt's Place are why. The Chicago deep dish chapter of this record is thick in this part of the country, and the meatball pie is its most argued-over page.

Ask around Morton Grove and Burt's Place regulars will tell you the same. The meatball pizza also carries a history the fancier pies do not: it is the Italian-American kitchen meeting the pizzeria head-on — Sunday sauce meat, baked in a ball, meeting the coal oven's crust. When a shop serves it proudly, it is serving its own family's cooking in public.
