Ammazza

Home: Atlanta, Georgia
Style: Neapolitan
The meatball: crumbled, Old Fourth Ward
Signature pie: Meatball pie

Ammazza (Atlanta, GA) keeps the Neapolitan faith — and proves it with meatballs, the topping that exposes a lazy kitchen faster than any other.

Ammazza works to that standard in Atlanta — and asks to be judged by it. The Neapolitan school is a discipline: a soft, leopard-spotted crust from a fierce oven, San Marzano-style tomato, and restraint everywhere. Meatballs here are used sparingly, the way Naples would insist. That is how Ammazza keeps it in Atlanta.

The meatball itself comes crumbled, Old Fourth Ward: the house meatballs are broken over the pie in rough crumbles, so the seasoning — garlic, parsley, pecorino worked into the grind — distributes through every slice instead of arriving in lumps. In Atlanta, Ammazza would not serve it any other way.

The pie to know is Meatball pie. Everything else on the menu orbits it here; this is the order that fills the room's recommendation when a stranger asks what to get. That is how Ammazza keeps it in Atlanta.

The clientele is the cross-section every great pizzeria collects: families on a Friday, regulars with a standing order, and first-timers sent by somebody who insisted. In Atlanta, Ammazza would not serve it any other way.

At Ammazza in Atlanta, the point deserves underlining. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.
