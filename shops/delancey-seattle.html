Delancey

Home: Seattle, Washington
Style: Wood-fired
The meatball: crumbled, Ballard's destination oven
Signature pie: Meatball pie

Ask regulars in Seattle where the meatball pizza matters and Delancey comes up — a Wood-fired shop whose signature, Meatball pie, earned this page.

Delancey works to that standard in Seattle — and asks to be judged by it. A wood oven cooks fast and uneven on purpose: the crust puffs, spots, and perfumes with smoke, and toppings must be built to survive ninety fierce seconds. Seattle has other pies; Delancey stakes its name on this one.

Here the meatball is crumbled, Ballard's destination oven. Crumbling is the generous method: meat in every bite, edges that crisp against the cheese, and a pie that reads as a meatball pie from edge to tip rather than a cheese pie with guests. In Seattle, Delancey would not serve it any other way.

Meatball pie is the signature — the pie that carries the house meatball at full strength, built on the crust the shop is known for and finished the way the regulars expect. It is the Delancey way in Seattle, and the regulars hold them to it.

There is no theatre here beyond the oven's — the work is visible, the menu is short enough to trust, and the meatball pie does the talking. It is the Delancey way in Seattle, and the regulars hold them to it.

There is a reason Delancey draws its Seattle following. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.

On WA's pizza map, Seattle is not a pass-through — it is a pie town, and rooms like Delancey are why. The Wood-fired chapter of this record is thick in this part of the country, and the meatball pie is its most argued-over page.

Ask around Seattle and Delancey regulars will tell you the same. The meatball pizza also carries a history the fancier pies do not: it is the Italian-American kitchen meeting the pizzeria head-on — Sunday sauce meat, baked in a ball, meeting the coal oven's crust. When a shop serves it proudly, it is serving its own family's cooking in public.

That is the whole of the Delancey entry: a city, a style, a meatball, a pie. In this record, that is what a pizzeria is.
