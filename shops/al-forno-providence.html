Al Forno

Home: Providence, Rhode Island
Style: Grilled pizza
The meatball: crumbled over the grilled original
Signature pie: Grilled meatball pizza
On the record since: 1980

The directory files Al Forno of Providence, RI under Grilled pizza — and files it there because of one pie: Grilled meatball pizza.

That is the bar Al Forno sets for itself in Providence, pie after pie. Grilled pizza cooks the dough directly over fire, flipped once, topped lightly — the technique Al Forno invented in Providence and the country adopted. It is the Al Forno way in Providence, and the regulars hold them to it.

Here the meatball is crumbled over the grilled original. Crumbling is the generous method: meat in every bite, edges that crisp against the cheese, and a pie that reads as a meatball pie from edge to tip rather than a cheese pie with guests. In Providence, Al Forno would not serve it any other way.

The pie to know is Grilled meatball pizza. Everything else on the menu orbits it here; this is the order that fills the room's recommendation when a stranger asks what to get. Al Forno keeps that standard in Providence, night after night.

On the record since 1980. Johanne Killeen and George Germon opened Al Forno in 1980 and invented grilled pizza — dough cooked directly over wood coals — a technique copied nationwide.

At Al Forno in Providence, the point deserves underlining. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.

On RI's pizza map, Providence is not a pass-through — it is a pie town, and rooms like Al Forno are why. The Grilled pizza chapter of this record is thick in this part of the country, and the meatball pie is its most argued-over page.

There is a reason Al Forno draws its Providence following. The meatball pizza also carries a history the fancier pies do not: it is the Italian-American kitchen meeting the pizzeria head-on — Sunday sauce meat, baked in a ball, meeting the coal oven's crust. When a shop serves it proudly, it is serving its own family's cooking in public.

That is the whole of the Al Forno entry: a city, a style, a meatball, a pie. In this record, that is what a pizzeria is.
