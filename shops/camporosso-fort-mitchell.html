Camporosso

Home: Fort Mitchell, Kentucky
Style: Neapolitan
The meatball: crumbled
Signature pie: Meatball pie

Camporosso (Fort Mitchell, KY) keeps the Neapolitan faith — and proves it with meatballs, the topping that exposes a lazy kitchen faster than any other.

At Camporosso in Fort Mitchell, that standard is not theory — it is the nightly measure. The Neapolitan school is a discipline: a soft, leopard-spotted crust from a fierce oven, San Marzano-style tomato, and restraint everywhere. Meatballs here are used sparingly, the way Naples would insist. It is the Camporosso way in Fort Mitchell, and the regulars hold them to it.

The preparation is crumbled — the kitchen treats its meatball mix as a topping in its own right, crumbling it over the sauce so the pie eats like a baked meatball parmigiana in slice form. Fort Mitchell has other pies; Camporosso stakes its name on this one.

The pie to know is Meatball pie. Everything else on the menu orbits it here; this is the order that fills the room's recommendation when a stranger asks what to get. It is the Camporosso way in Fort Mitchell, and the regulars hold them to it.

Shops like this one hold a neighborhood together: the birthday pie, the Friday pie, the pie after the game. The meatball version is the connoisseur's standing order among them. That is how Camporosso keeps it in Fort Mitchell.

Camporosso earns its place on documented proof — press, awards, and the following a great meatball pie collects. No shop pays to be here; the pie pays its way.
