Marco's Coal Fired

Home: Denver, Colorado
Style: Neapolitan
The meatball: crumbled, coal-fired Neapolitan
Signature pie: Meatball pie

Marco's Coal Fired works in the Neapolitan tradition in Denver, CO, and its meatball pie is the house examination: grind, seasoning, crumb, and crust all graded at once.

It is the standard Marco's Coal Fired was built on in Denver, and the one its regulars enforce. The Neapolitan school is a discipline: a soft, leopard-spotted crust from a fierce oven, San Marzano-style tomato, and restraint everywhere. Meatballs here are used sparingly, the way Naples would insist. It is the Marco's Coal Fired way in Denver, and the regulars hold them to it.

The preparation is crumbled, coal-fired Neapolitan — the kitchen treats its meatball mix as a topping in its own right, crumbling it over the sauce so the pie eats like a baked meatball parmigiana in slice form. In Denver, Marco's Coal Fired would not serve it any other way.

Meatball pie is the signature — the pie that carries the house meatball at full strength, built on the crust the shop is known for and finished the way the regulars expect. Marco's Coal Fired keeps that standard in Denver, night after night.

There is no theatre here beyond the oven's — the work is visible, the menu is short enough to trust, and the meatball pie does the talking. That is how Marco's Coal Fired keeps it in Denver.

That is the whole of the Marco's Coal Fired entry: a city, a style, a meatball, a pie. In this record, that is what a pizzeria is.
