Mozza Pizzeria

Home: Los Angeles, California
Style: California artisan
The meatball: crumbled with ricotta, Nancy Silverton crust
Signature pie: Meatball and ricotta pie

The directory files Mozza Pizzeria of Los Angeles, CA under California artisan — and files it there because of one pie: Meatball and ricotta pie.

That is the bar Mozza Pizzeria sets for itself in Los Angeles, pie after pie. The California school treats the pie as a plate: farmers-market produce, inventive sauces, and a crust with real fermentation behind it. Mozza Pizzeria keeps that standard in Los Angeles, night after night.

The meatball itself comes crumbled with ricotta, Nancy Silverton crust: the house meatballs are broken over the pie in rough crumbles, so the seasoning — garlic, parsley, pecorino worked into the grind — distributes through every slice instead of arriving in lumps. In Los Angeles, Mozza Pizzeria would not serve it any other way.

Meatball and ricotta pie is the signature — the pie that carries the house meatball at full strength, built on the crust the shop is known for and finished the way the regulars expect. In Los Angeles, Mozza Pizzeria would not serve it any other way.

Shops like this one hold a neighborhood together: the birthday pie, the Friday pie, the pie after the game. The meatball version is the connoisseur's standing order among them. That is how Mozza Pizzeria keeps it in Los Angeles.

There is a reason Mozza Pizzeria draws its Los Angeles following. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.

On CA's pizza map, Los Angeles is not a pass-through — it is a pie town, and rooms like Mozza Pizzeria are why. The California artisan chapter of this record is thick in this part of the country, and the meatball pie is its most argued-over page.

Ask around Los Angeles and Mozza Pizzeria regulars will tell you the same. The meatball pizza also carries a history the fancier pies do not: it is the Italian-American kitchen meeting the pizzeria head-on — Sunday sauce meat, baked in a ball, meeting the coal oven's crust. When a shop serves it proudly, it is serving its own family's cooking in public.
