Adelina's

Home: Brooklyn, New York
Style: Wood-fired
The meatball: crumbled, Greenpoint Italian
Signature pie: Meatball pie

The record of Wood-fired pizza in NY runs through rooms like Adelina's in Brooklyn, where the meatball pie is treated as the main event rather than an option.

Adelina's in Brooklyn is where that standard gets graded, slice by slice. A wood oven cooks fast and uneven on purpose: the crust puffs, spots, and perfumes with smoke, and toppings must be built to survive ninety fierce seconds. In Brooklyn, Adelina's would not serve it any other way.

The meatball itself comes crumbled, Greenpoint Italian: the house meatballs are broken over the pie in rough crumbles, so the seasoning — garlic, parsley, pecorino worked into the grind — distributes through every slice instead of arriving in lumps. In Brooklyn, Adelina's would not serve it any other way.

The signature is Meatball pie. On the menu it may sit among a dozen pies, but in this record it is the one that counts: the pie the kitchen's meatball was born for. In Brooklyn, Adelina's would not serve it any other way.

The room runs the way good pizzerias do: a counter, an oven you can watch, boxes stacked for the takeout trade, and a rhythm that peaks at dinner and never quite empties. That is how Adelina's keeps it in Brooklyn.

At Adelina's in Brooklyn, the point deserves underlining. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.
