Santillo's Brick Oven Pizza

Home: Elizabeth, New Jersey
Style: Coal-fired
The meatball: sliced, baked to your number
Signature pie: Meatball pie, Santillo's style
On the record since: 1918

Ask regulars in Elizabeth where the meatball pizza matters and Santillo's Brick Oven Pizza comes up — a Coal-fired shop whose signature, Meatball pie, Santillo's style, earned this page.

It is the standard Santillo's Brick Oven Pizza was built on in Elizabeth, and the one its regulars enforce. Coal heat is the old New York fire — hotter and drier than wood, blistering a crust in minutes and leaving the signature char that whole generations grew up on. In Elizabeth, Santillo's Brick Oven Pizza would not serve it any other way.

The preparation is sliced, baked to your number. Slicing is a discipline: the meatballs are baked or simmered first, cooled, cut, and only then laid over the cheese, so the pie finishes them rather than depending on them. In Elizabeth, Santillo's Brick Oven Pizza would not serve it any other way.

Order Meatball pie, Santillo's style — the house meatball pie, and the reason for this entry. It is the plate the regulars judge first and the one new cooks learn before anything else. Santillo's Brick Oven Pizza keeps that standard in Elizabeth, night after night.

On the record since 1918. Lou Santillo set up in Elizabeth in 1918; grandson Al Santillo runs the great brick oven today, baking every pie to a doneness number the customer calls.

The Santillo's Brick Oven Pizza kitchen in Elizabeth treats this as doctrine. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.

On NJ's pizza map, Elizabeth is not a pass-through — it is a pie town, and rooms like Santillo's Brick Oven Pizza are why. The Coal-fired chapter of this record is thick in this part of the country, and the meatball pie is its most argued-over page.

There is a reason Santillo's Brick Oven Pizza draws its Elizabeth following. The meatball pizza also carries a history the fancier pies do not: it is the Italian-American kitchen meeting the pizzeria head-on — Sunday sauce meat, baked in a ball, meeting the coal oven's crust. When a shop serves it proudly, it is serving its own family's cooking in public.
