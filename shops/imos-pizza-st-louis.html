Imo's Pizza

Home: St. Louis, Missouri
Style: St. Louis style
The meatball: crumbled, house style
Signature pie: Meatball pie, St. Louis style
On the record since: 1964

The directory files Imo's Pizza of St. Louis, MO under St. Louis style — and files it there because of one pie: Meatball pie, St. Louis style.

That is the bar Imo's Pizza sets for itself in St. Louis, pie after pie. St. Louis style rides on Provel — a processed blend of cheddar, Swiss, and provolone that melts like no other cheese — over a cracker crust, cut in squares and rectangles. In St. Louis, Imo's Pizza would not serve it any other way.

Here the meatball is crumbled, house style. Crumbling is the generous method: meat in every bite, edges that crisp against the cheese, and a pie that reads as a meatball pie from edge to tip rather than a cheese pie with guests. Imo's Pizza keeps that standard in St. Louis, night after night.

Meatball pie, St. Louis style is the signature — the pie that carries the house meatball at full strength, built on the crust the shop is known for and finished the way the regulars expect. St. Louis has other pies; Imo's Pizza stakes its name on this one.

On the record since 1964. Ed and Margie Imo opened in 1964 and built St. Louis style around Provel — the processed cheddar-Swiss-provolone blend only this city calls its own.

Ask around St. Louis and Imo's Pizza regulars will tell you the same. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.

On MO's pizza map, St. Louis is not a pass-through — it is a pie town, and rooms like Imo's Pizza are why. The St. Louis style chapter of this record is thick in this part of the country, and the meatball pie is its most argued-over page.

Ask around St. Louis and Imo's Pizza regulars will tell you the same. The meatball pizza also carries a history the fancier pies do not: it is the Italian-American kitchen meeting the pizzeria head-on — Sunday sauce meat, baked in a ball, meeting the coal oven's crust. When a shop serves it proudly, it is serving its own family's cooking in public.

The directory records menus and signatures, never a number on a tag — the pie endures; figures are weather. Imo's Pizza is filed under St. Louis, MO, in the St. Louis style chapter.
