Tacconelli's Pizza

Home: Philadelphia, Pennsylvania
Style: Tomato pie
The meatball: crumbled over the 1946 tomato pie
Signature pie: Meatball tomato pie

Tacconelli's Pizza works in the Tomato pie tradition in Philadelphia, PA, and its meatball pie is the house examination: grind, seasoning, crumb, and crust all graded at once.

At Tacconelli's Pizza in Philadelphia, that standard is not theory — it is the nightly measure. The tomato pie inverts the usual order — a light hand with cheese under a bright, chunky tomato layer — a Trenton and South Philadelphia creed. Tacconelli's Pizza keeps that standard in Philadelphia, night after night.

Here the meatball is crumbled over the 1946 tomato pie. Crumbling is the generous method: meat in every bite, edges that crisp against the cheese, and a pie that reads as a meatball pie from edge to tip rather than a cheese pie with guests. Philadelphia has other pies; Tacconelli's Pizza stakes its name on this one.

Inside, the choreography is familiar and exact — dough stretched to order, the oven tended like a pet, pies called out by name as they land. That is how Tacconelli's Pizza keeps it in Philadelphia.

Ask around Philadelphia and Tacconelli's Pizza regulars will tell you the same. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.

On PA's pizza map, Philadelphia is not a pass-through — it is a pie town, and rooms like Tacconelli's Pizza are why. The Tomato pie chapter of this record is thick in this part of the country, and the meatball pie is its most argued-over page.

At Tacconelli's Pizza in Philadelphia, the point deserves underlining. The meatball pizza also carries a history the fancier pies do not: it is the Italian-American kitchen meeting the pizzeria head-on — Sunday sauce meat, baked in a ball, meeting the coal oven's crust. When a shop serves it proudly, it is serving its own family's cooking in public.
