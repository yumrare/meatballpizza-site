Loui's Pizza

Home: Hazel Park, Michigan
Style: Detroit style
The meatball: crumbled, Hazel Park legend
Signature pie: Meatball square
On the record since: 1955

Loui's Pizza is Hazel Park's entry in the meatball pizza record — a Detroit style kitchen in MI where the test pie is the one topped with meatballs.

Loui's Pizza works to that standard in Hazel Park — and asks to be judged by it. Detroit style bakes in blue-steel pans to a frico edge — cheese crisped against the metal all the way around — with toppings carried to the very corner. That is how Loui's Pizza keeps it in Hazel Park.

Here the meatball is crumbled, Hazel Park legend. Crumbling is the generous method: meat in every bite, edges that crisp against the cheese, and a pie that reads as a meatball pie from edge to tip rather than a cheese pie with guests. In Hazel Park, Loui's Pizza would not serve it any other way.

Meatball square is the signature — the pie that carries the house meatball at full strength, built on the crust the shop is known for and finished the way the regulars expect. Loui's Pizza keeps that standard in Hazel Park, night after night.

On the record since 1955. Louis Tourtois, a Buddy's alumnus, opened Loui's in 1955; the Hazel Park room and its cheesy-cornered squares are Detroit royalty.

There is a reason Loui's Pizza draws its Hazel Park following. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.

On MI's pizza map, Hazel Park is not a pass-through — it is a pie town, and rooms like Loui's Pizza are why. The Detroit style chapter of this record is thick in this part of the country, and the meatball pie is its most argued-over page.

The Loui's Pizza kitchen in Hazel Park treats this as doctrine. The meatball pizza also carries a history the fancier pies do not: it is the Italian-American kitchen meeting the pizzeria head-on — Sunday sauce meat, baked in a ball, meeting the coal oven's crust. When a shop serves it proudly, it is serving its own family's cooking in public.
