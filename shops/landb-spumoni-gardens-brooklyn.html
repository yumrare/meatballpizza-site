Brooklyn, New York · Landmark Oven

L&B Spumoni Gardens

Home: Brooklyn, New York
Style: Sicilian
The meatball: crumbled over the famous upside-down square
Signature pie: Meatball Sicilian square
On the record since: 1939

In Brooklyn, NY, the meatball pizza at L&B Spumoni Gardens is the order that tells you what the kitchen is made of — a Sicilian pie judged by its hardest topping.

At L&B Spumoni Gardens in Brooklyn, that standard is not theory — it is the nightly measure. The Sicilian square is a bakery pie: a thick, olive-oil-crisped crust, sauce over or under the cheese depending on the house, and corners that regulars request by name. Brooklyn has other pies; L&B Spumoni Gardens stakes its name on this one.

The preparation is crumbled over the famous upside-down square — the kitchen treats its meatball mix as a topping in its own right, crumbling it over the sauce so the pie eats like a baked meatball parmigiana in slice form. It is the L&B Spumoni Gardens way in Brooklyn, and the regulars hold them to it.

Meatball Sicilian square is the signature — the pie that carries the house meatball at full strength, built on the crust the shop is known for and finished the way the regulars expect. In Brooklyn, L&B Spumoni Gardens would not serve it any other way.

On the record since 1939. Ludovico Barbati founded L&B in 1939; its upside-down Sicilian square — cheese under sauce — became one of Brooklyn's defining pies, spumoni to follow.

There is a reason L&B Spumoni Gardens draws its Brooklyn following. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.

On NY's pizza map, Brooklyn is not a pass-through — it is a pie town, and rooms like L&B Spumoni Gardens are why. The Sicilian chapter of this record is thick in this part of the country, and the meatball pie is its most argued-over page.

There is a reason L&B Spumoni Gardens draws its Brooklyn following. The meatball pizza also carries a history the fancier pies do not: it is the Italian-American kitchen meeting the pizzeria head-on — Sunday sauce meat, baked in a ball, meeting the coal oven's crust. When a shop serves it proudly, it is serving its own family's cooking in public.

That is the whole of the L&B Spumoni Gardens entry: a city, a style, a meatball, a pie. In this record, that is what a pizzeria is.
