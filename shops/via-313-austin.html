Via 313

Home: Austin, Texas
Style: Detroit style
The meatball: crumbled on Detroit squares
Signature pie: Meatball square
On the record since: 2011

Via 313 is Austin's entry in the meatball pizza record — a Detroit style kitchen in TX where the test pie is the one topped with meatballs.

That is the bar Via 313 sets for itself in Austin, pie after pie. Detroit style bakes in blue-steel pans to a frico edge — cheese crisped against the metal all the way around — with toppings carried to the very corner. It is the Via 313 way in Austin, and the regulars hold them to it.

The meatball itself comes crumbled on Detroit squares: the house meatballs are broken over the pie in rough crumbles, so the seasoning — garlic, parsley, pecorino worked into the grind — distributes through every slice instead of arriving in lumps. That is how Via 313 keeps it in Austin.

Meatball square is the signature — the pie that carries the house meatball at full strength, built on the crust the shop is known for and finished the way the regulars expect. That is how Via 313 keeps it in Austin.

On the record since 2011. Brothers Zane and Brandon Hunt began Detroit style in an Austin trailer in 2011 — one of the pies that carried the style nationwide.

There is a reason Via 313 draws its Austin following. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.

On TX's pizza map, Austin is not a pass-through — it is a pie town, and rooms like Via 313 are why. The Detroit style chapter of this record is thick in this part of the country, and the meatball pie is its most argued-over page.

Ask around Austin and Via 313 regulars will tell you the same. The meatball pizza also carries a history the fancier pies do not: it is the Italian-American kitchen meeting the pizzeria head-on — Sunday sauce meat, baked in a ball, meeting the coal oven's crust. When a shop serves it proudly, it is serving its own family's cooking in public.

That is the whole of the Via 313 entry: a city, a style, a meatball, a pie. In this record, that is what a pizzeria is.
