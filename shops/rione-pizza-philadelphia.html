Rione Pizza

Home: Philadelphia, Pennsylvania
Style: Roman
The meatball: crumbled over al taglio
Signature pie: Meatball al taglio

Ask regulars in Philadelphia where the meatball pizza matters and Rione Pizza comes up — a Roman shop whose signature, Meatball al taglio, earned this page.

At Rione Pizza in Philadelphia, that standard is not theory — it is the nightly measure. Roman style is the baker's pizza — long-fermented, cut with scissors, sold al taglio, with a crust full of air and crunch. In Philadelphia, Rione Pizza would not serve it any other way.

The preparation is crumbled over al taglio — the kitchen treats its meatball mix as a topping in its own right, crumbling it over the sauce so the pie eats like a baked meatball parmigiana in slice form. That is how Rione Pizza keeps it in Philadelphia.

The signature is Meatball al taglio. On the menu it may sit among a dozen pies, but in this record it is the one that counts: the pie the kitchen's meatball was born for. Philadelphia has other pies; Rione Pizza stakes its name on this one.

Shops like this one hold a neighborhood together: the birthday pie, the Friday pie, the pie after the game. The meatball version is the connoisseur's standing order among them. Rione Pizza keeps that standard in Philadelphia, night after night.

There is a reason Rione Pizza draws its Philadelphia following. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.
