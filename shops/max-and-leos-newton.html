Max & Leo's

Home: Newton, Massachusetts
Style: Coal-fired
The meatball: crumbled
Signature pie: Meatball pie

The record of Coal-fired pizza in MA runs through rooms like Max & Leo's in Newton, where the meatball pie is treated as the main event rather than an option.

At Max & Leo's in Newton, that standard is not theory — it is the nightly measure. Coal heat is the old New York fire — hotter and drier than wood, blistering a crust in minutes and leaving the signature char that whole generations grew up on. Newton has other pies; Max & Leo's stakes its name on this one.

The meatball itself comes crumbled: the house meatballs are broken over the pie in rough crumbles, so the seasoning — garlic, parsley, pecorino worked into the grind — distributes through every slice instead of arriving in lumps. It is the Max & Leo's way in Newton, and the regulars hold them to it.

The pie to know is Meatball pie. Everything else on the menu orbits it here; this is the order that fills the room's recommendation when a stranger asks what to get. That is how Max & Leo's keeps it in Newton.

There is no theatre here beyond the oven's — the work is visible, the menu is short enough to trust, and the meatball pie does the talking. That is how Max & Leo's keeps it in Newton.
