Manco & Manco Pizza

Home: Ocean City, New Jersey
Style: New York slice
The meatball: sliced
Signature pie: Meatball slice
On the record since: 1956

In Ocean City, NJ, the meatball pizza at Manco & Manco Pizza is the order that tells you what the kitchen is made of — a New York slice pie judged by its hardest topping.

Manco & Manco Pizza in Ocean City is where that standard gets graded, slice by slice. The New York school asks for a crust that folds without cracking, a sauce with a little brightness, and cheese that stretches rather than pools. On a slice this size, a meatball has nowhere to hide. Manco & Manco Pizza keeps that standard in Ocean City, night after night.

The preparation is sliced. Slicing is a discipline: the meatballs are baked or simmered first, cooled, cut, and only then laid over the cheese, so the pie finishes them rather than depending on them. Ocean City has other pies; Manco & Manco Pizza stakes its name on this one.

Order Meatball slice — the house meatball pie, and the reason for this entry. It is the plate the regulars judge first and the one new cooks learn before anything else. In Ocean City, Manco & Manco Pizza would not serve it any other way.

On the record since 1956. The Ocean City boardwalk institution traces to 1956 — the former Mack & Manco — and still anchors the boards' pizza argument.

The Manco & Manco Pizza kitchen in Ocean City treats this as doctrine. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.

On NJ's pizza map, Ocean City is not a pass-through — it is a pie town, and rooms like Manco & Manco Pizza are why. The New York slice chapter of this record is thick in this part of the country, and the meatball pie is its most argued-over page.

Ask around Ocean City and Manco & Manco Pizza regulars will tell you the same. The meatball pizza also carries a history the fancier pies do not: it is the Italian-American kitchen meeting the pizzeria head-on — Sunday sauce meat, baked in a ball, meeting the coal oven's crust. When a shop serves it proudly, it is serving its own family's cooking in public.

That is the whole of the Manco & Manco Pizza entry: a city, a style, a meatball, a pie. In this record, that is what a pizzeria is.
