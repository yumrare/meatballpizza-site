2 Amys

Home: Washington, District of Columbia
Style: Neapolitan
The meatball: crumbled, D.O.C. certified pies
Signature pie: Meatball pie

The directory files 2 Amys of Washington, DC under Neapolitan — and files it there because of one pie: Meatball pie.

That is the bar 2 Amys sets for itself in Washington, pie after pie. The Neapolitan school is a discipline: a soft, leopard-spotted crust from a fierce oven, San Marzano-style tomato, and restraint everywhere. Meatballs here are used sparingly, the way Naples would insist. Washington has other pies; 2 Amys stakes its name on this one.

Here the meatball is crumbled, D.O.C. certified pies. Crumbling is the generous method: meat in every bite, edges that crisp against the cheese, and a pie that reads as a meatball pie from edge to tip rather than a cheese pie with guests. 2 Amys keeps that standard in Washington, night after night.

The signature is Meatball pie. On the menu it may sit among a dozen pies, but in this record it is the one that counts: the pie the kitchen's meatball was born for. In Washington, 2 Amys would not serve it any other way.

Shops like this one hold a neighborhood together: the birthday pie, the Friday pie, the pie after the game. The meatball version is the connoisseur's standing order among them. In Washington, 2 Amys would not serve it any other way.

The 2 Amys kitchen in Washington treats this as doctrine. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.

On DC's pizza map, Washington is not a pass-through — it is a pie town, and rooms like 2 Amys are why. The Neapolitan chapter of this record is thick in this part of the country, and the meatball pie is its most argued-over page.

At 2 Amys in Washington, the point deserves underlining. The meatball pizza also carries a history the fancier pies do not: it is the Italian-American kitchen meeting the pizzeria head-on — Sunday sauce meat, baked in a ball, meeting the coal oven's crust. When a shop serves it proudly, it is serving its own family's cooking in public.

That is the whole of the 2 Amys entry: a city, a style, a meatball, a pie. In this record, that is what a pizzeria is.
