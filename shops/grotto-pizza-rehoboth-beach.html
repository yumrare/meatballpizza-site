Grotto Pizza

Home: Rehoboth Beach, Delaware
Style: New York slice
The meatball: sliced
Signature pie: Meatball slice
On the record since: 1960

The directory files Grotto Pizza of Rehoboth Beach, DE under New York slice — and files it there because of one pie: Meatball slice.

Regulars in Rehoboth Beach hold Grotto Pizza to exactly that standard. The New York school asks for a crust that folds without cracking, a sauce with a little brightness, and cheese that stretches rather than pools. On a slice this size, a meatball has nowhere to hide. In Rehoboth Beach, Grotto Pizza would not serve it any other way.

The preparation is sliced. Slicing is a discipline: the meatballs are baked or simmered first, cooled, cut, and only then laid over the cheese, so the pie finishes them rather than depending on them. Grotto Pizza keeps that standard in Rehoboth Beach, night after night.

The signature is Meatball slice. On the menu it may sit among a dozen pies, but in this record it is the one that counts: the pie the kitchen's meatball was born for. Grotto Pizza keeps that standard in Rehoboth Beach, night after night.

On the record since 1960. Dominick Pulieri opened the first Grotto in 1960; its swirled-sauce pie became Delaware's defining slice, beach town by beach town.

Ask around Rehoboth Beach and Grotto Pizza regulars will tell you the same. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.

On DE's pizza map, Rehoboth Beach is not a pass-through — it is a pie town, and rooms like Grotto Pizza are why. The New York slice chapter of this record is thick in this part of the country, and the meatball pie is its most argued-over page.

The Grotto Pizza kitchen in Rehoboth Beach treats this as doctrine. The meatball pizza also carries a history the fancier pies do not: it is the Italian-American kitchen meeting the pizzeria head-on — Sunday sauce meat, baked in a ball, meeting the coal oven's crust. When a shop serves it proudly, it is serving its own family's cooking in public.

That is the whole of the Grotto Pizza entry: a city, a style, a meatball, a pie. In this record, that is what a pizzeria is.
