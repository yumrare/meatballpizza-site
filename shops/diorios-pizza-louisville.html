Louisville, Kentucky · Landmark Oven

Diorio's Pizza

Home: Louisville, Kentucky
Style: New York slice
The meatball: sliced thin on a blistered crust
Signature pie: Meatball slice
On the record since: 1932

Ask regulars in Louisville where the meatball pizza matters and Diorio's Pizza comes up — a New York slice shop whose signature, Meatball slice, earned this page.

Diorio's Pizza works to that standard in Louisville — and asks to be judged by it. The New York school asks for a crust that folds without cracking, a sauce with a little brightness, and cheese that stretches rather than pools. On a slice this size, a meatball has nowhere to hide. Louisville has other pies; Diorio's Pizza stakes its name on this one.

The meatball itself comes sliced thin on a blistered crust: cooked meatballs are sliced so they lie flat on the pie, crisp at the edges in the oven, and give a little meat in every bite instead of a boulder in some. It is the classic slice-shop solution, and it works because the ratio never lies. Diorio's Pizza keeps that standard in Louisville, night after night.

Meatball slice is the signature — the pie that carries the house meatball at full strength, built on the crust the shop is known for and finished the way the regulars expect. It is the Diorio's Pizza way in Louisville, and the regulars hold them to it.

On the record since 1932. Diorio's has served Louisville slices since 1932, one of Kentucky's oldest pizza names.

Ask around Louisville and Diorio's Pizza regulars will tell you the same. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.

On KY's pizza map, Louisville is not a pass-through — it is a pie town, and rooms like Diorio's Pizza are why. The New York slice chapter of this record is thick in this part of the country, and the meatball pie is its most argued-over page.

At Diorio's Pizza in Louisville, the point deserves underlining. The meatball pizza also carries a history the fancier pies do not: it is the Italian-American kitchen meeting the pizzeria head-on — Sunday sauce meat, baked in a ball, meeting the coal oven's crust. When a shop serves it proudly, it is serving its own family's cooking in public.

That is the whole of the Diorio's Pizza entry: a city, a style, a meatball, a pie. In this record, that is what a pizzeria is.
