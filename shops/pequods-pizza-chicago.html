Pequod's Pizza

Home: Chicago, Illinois
Style: Chicago deep dish
The meatball: crumbled under the caramelized crust ring
Signature pie: Meatball pan pie
On the record since: 1992

In Chicago, IL, the meatball pizza at Pequod's Pizza is the order that tells you what the kitchen is made of — a Chicago deep dish pie judged by its hardest topping.

Pequod's Pizza works to that standard in Chicago — and asks to be judged by it. Deep dish is a construction, not a crust with ambitions: a buttery pan walls in layers of cheese and filling under chunky tomato, and it is eaten with a knife and fork without apology. Chicago has other pies; Pequod's Pizza stakes its name on this one.

The meatball itself comes crumbled under the caramelized crust ring: the house meatballs are broken over the pie in rough crumbles, so the seasoning — garlic, parsley, pecorino worked into the grind — distributes through every slice instead of arriving in lumps. That is how Pequod's Pizza keeps it in Chicago.

Meatball pan pie is the signature — the pie that carries the house meatball at full strength, built on the crust the shop is known for and finished the way the regulars expect. Chicago has other pies; Pequod's Pizza stakes its name on this one.

On the record since 1992. Burt Katz opened Pequod's in 1992 after his Gulliver's and Burt's Place years; the caramelized cheese-to-the-edge pan crust is his signature.

There is a reason Pequod's Pizza draws its Chicago following. Why does the meatball pie matter enough to anchor a national record? Because it grades everything at once. The grind shows in the texture. The seasoning shows in the first bite. The breadcrumb and the milk — or their absence — show in the tenderness. A kitchen can hide behind a fashionable crust or a rare cheese; it cannot hide behind a meatball.

On IL's pizza map, Chicago is not a pass-through — it is a pie town, and rooms like Pequod's Pizza are why. The Chicago deep dish chapter of this record is thick in this part of the country, and the meatball pie is its most argued-over page.

At Pequod's Pizza in Chicago, the point deserves underlining. The meatball pizza also carries a history the fancier pies do not: it is the Italian-American kitchen meeting the pizzeria head-on — Sunday sauce meat, baked in a ball, meeting the coal oven's crust. When a shop serves it proudly, it is serving its own family's cooking in public.

That is the whole of the Pequod's Pizza entry: a city, a style, a meatball, a pie. In this record, that is what a pizzeria is.
